RESEARCH QUESTION: How do the synthetic-data ratio and the optimization algorithm
affect predictive performance and degradation across generations?

Baseline (G0, 100% real data): test F1 = 0.631 (HC), 0.631 (SA)

1) RATIO EFFECT - mean Collapse Index over G1-G10 (positive = worse than baseline)
   HC: 90/10 +4.7%,  70/30 +7.8%,  50/50 +10.0%
   SA: 90/10 +1.9%,  70/30 +5.3%,  50/50 +7.7%
   -> For both algorithms, a larger synthetic share led to more degradation.
   -> Every Collapse Index value (all series, G1-G10) lies between -0.6% and +18.4%.

2) ALGORITHM EFFECT - Hill Climbing vs Simulated Annealing at each ratio
   90/10:  mean val F1  HC 0.583 vs SA 0.600;   mean CI  HC +4.7% vs SA +1.9%
   70/30:  mean val F1  HC 0.564 vs SA 0.582;   mean CI  HC +7.8% vs SA +5.3%
   50/50:  mean val F1  HC 0.552 vs SA 0.569;   mean CI  HC +10.0% vs SA +7.7%
   -> Over the 30 synthetic runs, SA found a higher validation F1 than HC in 14, the same in 8, and a lower one in 8.
   -> Average evaluations used: HC 20.8, SA 26.5 (budget 40).
   -> HC stopped at a local optimum (with budget left over) in 31 of 31 runs.
   -> The largest Collapse Index (+18.4%) is HC at G7 50/50; SA on the same training data scored +8.2%.
      Same training data, so about 10.3 points of that spike come from the search, not the data.
